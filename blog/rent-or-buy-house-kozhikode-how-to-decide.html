Rent or Buy a House in Kozhikode: How to Decide

By the Kalathil Properties team, Kozhikode · 8 November 2026 · 5 min read

Almost everyone who looks at property in Kozhikode asks the same thing at some point: should I rent for now, or buy and be done with it? There is no single right answer. The useful question is which choice fits your next few years, your cash and your plans.

Start with how long you will really stay

Buying has costs that only pay off if you stay long enough. If you are not sure you will be in Kozhikode in three or four years, because of a job, treatment, a child's school or a move abroad, renting keeps you free to leave. If you already know this is where the family will live for a long time, buying starts to make more sense.

Count the full cost of buying, not just the price

The price on the board is only part of it. Add registration and stamp duty, legal checks, any broker fee, repairs you may want to do before moving in, and yearly property tax. Rates and rules change, so ask a lawyer or chartered accountant for the current figures instead of trusting an old article, including this one. Then add the money you will not have in your bank while it sits in a house.

Count the full cost of renting too

Rent is easier to see, but it is not the whole bill. Ask about the deposit, who pays for repairs, whether electricity and water are included, and how much the rent can rise when the agreement is renewed. A furnished home saves you buying furniture, which matters if the stay may be short. Flexibility has a price, and so does being tied down

Renting costs you certainty. The owner can sell, the rent can go up, and you cannot change the walls. Buying costs you flexibility. If your plans change, selling a house takes time and is not always quick. Neither choice is free. You are deciding which kind of risk you can live with.

If you buy, check the paperwork before you fall for the house

Ask a lawyer to look at the documents before you pay an advance. Typically they will want to see the title deed and the chain of ownership, an encumbrance certificate, recent tax receipts and, for a built house, the building permit and the completion or occupancy certificate. Make sure the plot on the ground matches the plot on paper. This is general information, not legal advice, and the exact list depends on the property, so ask your lawyer what applies to yours.

A small thing that confuses many buyers: cents

In Kerala, land is usually sold in cents. One cent is about 435.6 square feet, and 100 cents make an acre. So a 2.5 cent plot is about 1,090 square feet of land. The built-up area of a house is a different number, so always ask which one a seller is quoting.

If you live abroad

Many buyers are in the Gulf or elsewhere and cannot visit often. Video tours, a trusted person who can check the property in person, and clear written updates help a great deal. If you already own a home here, you can also rent it out and have it looked after instead of selling. Our property management service covers tenant search, rent follow-up and inspections for owners.

A simple way to decide

Write down three things: how long you are likely to stay, how much cash you can comfortably tie up, and what you would do if your plans changed in a year. If the answers are "not sure", "not much" and "move", rent. If they are "many years", "enough to spare" and "stay anyway", buying deserves a serious look.

Common questions

How big is one cent of land in Kerala?

Should I rent or buy if I am not sure how long I will stay?

Renting usually keeps you more flexible, because buying has costs that only make sense if you stay for some years. Think about how long you are likely to stay, how much cash you can tie up, and what you would do if your plans changed.

Do I need a lawyer to buy a house in Kozhikode?
